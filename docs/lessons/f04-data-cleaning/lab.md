## The scenario

Riverside Evening Academy has finished its spring term. The registrar exported one
row per enrollment, and the student support office wants to invite about 120
students to a free tutoring program next term. They have no model and no analyst.
They have you, a synthetic table, and one rule from their director: the export
must not be "cleaned" by throwing away a quarter of the students.

This is a different dataset from the notebook. It comes from
`datasets/foundations/student_performance_messy.csv`, produced by the course
generator with its own seed. It has roughly 500 unique enrollments, more missing
values, inconsistent course and tutoring labels, and duplicate rows. The notebook
used 328 rows and seed `7`; nothing about this file matches those numbers.

Your job is to turn the export into a model-ready table, measure what each cleaning
decision costs or buys, and recommend a flagging rule that fits the support
office's capacity — without producing a per-student dossier.

!!! warning "This table is synthetic"
    Every row was generated by a documented script. No real student, family, or
    school is represented. That makes the exercise safe to run, and it also limits
    what you may conclude: your results say nothing about real students, real
    tutoring, or real pass rates. State that limitation in your report instead of
    implying otherwise.

## Why this matters

Cleaning decisions are policy decisions in disguise. Dropping incomplete rows
removes a group of students who may be the ones who need help. Filling a missing
attendance value with the median invents attendance that never happened. Flagging
the bottom 120 students turns a statistical score into an invitation that some
students receive and others do not.

The technical work here is not hard. The discipline is: write the decision first,
measure its effect, and keep the output aggregate enough that it cannot be used to
label a person.

## Constraints

Your cleaning must satisfy all five:

1. **Keep at least 95% of unique rows.** That is 475 of the roughly 500 unique
   enrollments. Complete-case deletion cannot meet this constraint, and that is
   intentional: you must impute and defend it.
2. **Drop no row except exact duplicates.** No filtering by score, attendance, or
   outcome.
3. **Normalize labels explicitly.** `course` must end up in
   `{Math, Physics, CS, Unknown}` and `tutoring` in `{yes, no, unknown}`. Every row
   that falls outside the mapping must be counted, not dropped.
4. **Add missing indicators.** Each imputed numeric column gets a companion binary
   column recording that the value was missing.
5. **Export aggregates only.** No per-student CSV, no ranked list, no
   `student_id`-like column in any submitted file. The support office gets counts
   by group and a threshold recommendation, not names.

## Required steps

1. **Generate, fingerprint, and load.** Run the generator, compute the SHA-256 of
   the messy CSV, and load it. Record the command, seed, row count, and hash.
2. **Profile before touching anything.** Print the shape, the missing count per
   column, `df.duplicated().sum()`, and the raw value counts of `course` and
   `tutoring`. **Validation check 1:** assert that the raw table has duplicate rows
   and missing values, and assert that `course` contains at least one value that is
   not in `{Math, Physics, CS}`. Save the profile to `outputs/profile_before.csv`.
3. **Write the specification first.** `cleaning_spec.md` must list each decision,
   its reason, and the expected effect (rows, columns, or value set). Write it
   before you code the transformations; the report will compare it with what
   actually happened.
4. **Deduplicate.** Drop exact duplicates only. **Validation check 2:** assert that
   duplicates are now zero, that the row count fell by exactly the number you
   counted in step 2, and that the unique-row count still meets the 95% floor.
5. **Normalize the labels.** Strip whitespace, lowercase, map to canonical values,
   and route everything else to `Unknown` or `unknown`. Assert the resulting value
   sets and assert that the row count did not change. Record how many rows landed
   in each unknown bucket.
6. **Impute with indicators.** Build a `ColumnTransformer` with a numeric pipeline
   (`SimpleImputer(strategy="median", add_indicator=True)` then
   `StandardScaler`) and a categorical pipeline (`SimpleImputer(strategy=
   "most_frequent")` then `OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore",
   sparse_output=False)`). Assert that the transformed matrix has no NaN, that its
   row count equals the deduplicated count, and that its column count equals your
   hand calculation. State the calculation in the report.
7. **Measure every decision.** Compare four variants on **identical folds**: (a)
   the full specification, (b) imputation without indicators, (c) mean imputation
   instead of median, (d) complete-case deletion. Use one `StratifiedKFold`
   object with a fixed seed. **Validation check 3:** assert that all four variants
   score on the same test indices. Write `outputs/ablation.csv` with columns
   `variant, rows, mean_accuracy, std_accuracy, meets_95_percent_floor`.
8. **Choose a flagging rule.** The support office can serve 120 students out of
   about 500, so a 0.5 probability threshold will not fit. Fit a logistic
   regression on the cleaned table, predict each student's probability of passing,
   and flag the students with the **lowest** probabilities. Choose the probability
   cutoff that flags as close to 120 students as possible without exceeding it.
   Report the cutoff, the number flagged, the flagged share, and one sentence on
   who is likely to be missed because of that capacity limit.
9. **Check the groups.** Report the flagged share by `course` and by `tutoring` at
   your chosen cutoff. Flag any group whose share differs from the overall share by
   more than 15 percentage points, and say whether the difference is a property of
   the data or of your cutoff. Keep every table aggregate.
10. **Write the report and prove reproduction.** Delete `outputs/`, rerun your
    script or notebook from the top, and confirm the same artifacts reappear with
    the same values. Include the two commands and the file list in `README.md`.

## Constraints and safety

- Use only the generated synthetic table. Do not substitute a real school export,
  even anonymized, and do not add personal details to make the scenario realistic.
- Do not submit an individual ranking, a list of flagged students, or a per-student
  probability. Aggregate tables only.
- Do not use the outcome `final_score` as a feature when predicting `passed`; it is
  the term result and would leak the answer.
- Report every group result with its group size, and do not compare a group of 20
  with a group of 300 as if the difference were meaningful.
- State that imputed values are estimates, not measurements, and that the missing
  indicators exist because you cannot recover the truth.
- Do not describe the pilot as evidence that tutoring works. The table is
  synthetic and no intervention was run.
- Disclose substantive AI assistance in `reflection.md`.
